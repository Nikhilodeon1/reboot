**Question 1: what shows Type 4 as an observed failure?** Nothing does. Type 4 is a process hazard, and we did not observe it as a failure of a shipped detector. Section 6.4 now says so. The evidence is counterfactual. We pre-registered a rule for choosing between two variants of detector 5. The alternative had higher recall at demonstration scale and was tempting. The pre-registered criterion rejected it. One case with unknown ground truth was excluded from scoring in advance, and it is the only case on which the variants diverge: scored, it alone would have decided the comparison. A separate arm that fitted detector 3's recognition vocabulary to the corpus did not help. We also did not move the detector 1 threshold after seeing the near-miss. This shows what pre-registration prevented, not an error that occurred, and we do not offer it as more than that.

**Question 2: when is a threshold or a control clinically meaningful?** Section 6.2 now answers this. The 0.60 threshold is a conventional agreement benchmark with no clinical derivation. A negative control is informative when it is a pair of operationalizations that a domain expert accepts as interchangeable. A threshold is meaningful only if it sits clear of the legitimate-variation band of such pairs. For detector 1 the same implementation pair gives kappa {{N:d1_pop}} on MIMIC-IV and {{N:d1_pop_eicu}} on eICU-CRD, so the band is wide relative to the margin of 0.002 by which the MIMIC control misses the threshold. We state this as a prescription for building new detectors, to be fixed before seeing data. We do not apply it retroactively to detector 1, because moving its threshold now would be the Type 4 error.
