**What we heard.** Three points recur across the reviews. The abstract led with detector precision and recall that rest on a handful of cases. The catalogue was presented as more general and better supported than the evidence allows. The detectors were checked more thinly than the paper implied. We agree with all three. We ran the experiments you asked for. Each was pre-registered in a committed document before it ran, and the commit order is in Appendix B and in the 4open.science repository. Several results went against us.

**Corrections.** These are our errors, found while running the new experiments.
1. Detector 2's effect is confounded with corpus size at full scale, by our own pre-registered rule. A control that adds extra source stays instead of target stays recovers {{N:r2r1_full5}} of the shipped detector's effect at 5% leakage and {{N:r2r1_full20}} at 20%. At demonstration scale the ratios were {{N:r2r1_demo5}} and {{N:r2r1_demo20}}. Our line was 0.5. Target exposure still adds beyond volume (paired t = {{N:t_a1a2_full}}). We did not change the detector.
2. The statement that the near-miss worsened with more data is retracted. It compared a cohort-bootstrap flag probability at demonstration scale ({{N:d1_cohort_demo}}) with an audit-draw probability at full scale ({{N:d1_audit_full}}). Like for like, the cohort-bootstrap value is {{N:d1_cohort_demo}} and {{N:d1_cohort_full}}. Full scale shows that the population kappa ({{N:d1_pop}}) sits on the 0.60 threshold, so a 500-patient audit is close to a coin flip.
3. Detector 2's 0% control compared an arm with itself, so its true negative held by construction. A 20-replicate null we then ran gave {{N:null_flags}} flagged (exact 95% interval {{N:null_ci}}). That is consistent with the nominal 5%, and the upper bound is 25%.
4. The claim that leakage shifts downstream performance in a consistent direction is removed. At full scale no level is distinguishable from zero. On a large held-out demonstration target only 100% leakage is, at {{N:a5p_100}}.
5. The claim that the falsy-as-clean pattern concentrates in verdict-producing code is removed. It is reachable and unguarded in {{N:t4_narrow}} of applicable sites, or {{N:t4_broad}} if NaNs that arise only from diverged training count. We did not fix that convention in advance. The split between verdict code and other code reverses with it ({{N:t4_split_narrow}}; {{N:t4_split_broad}}), so we found no evidence of concentration.

**Other new evidence.** Detector 3: of {{N:t3_sampled}} hand-labelled selection sites, {{N:t3_labels}}. Sensitivity is undefined, and the detector returned indeterminate on {{N:t3_indet}} files. Yang et al.'s tool, built after three environment changes, reports nothing on both of our fixtures. Detector 5: the flagship composition ratio straddles its gate, flagged {{N:d5_1200}} and {{N:d5_4000}}, and the Type 2 model gives flag probabilities of about {{N:d5_pred1200}} and {{N:d5_pred4000}}, consistent with those counts. Baselines now use the same trained models, at both scales. Detector 1 observed at full scale: margins and kappa are in Section 6.2 and Table 2.

**Not done.** Missingness arms at full scale (the available caches lack the raw series), so that question is open there. No GPU work: the credentialed data stayed on one author's machine. The spot-check is an agreement figure, not a blind relabel: {{SPOTCHECK}}. The scans cover Python files only ({{N:t3_gap}}). Third-party code ran only in Docker.

**What we concede.** The catalogue is initial and incomplete. Types 2 to 4 have no external instance. Detector validation is thin and heterogeneous. The same group built and audited everything. If the committee judges Types 2 to 4 better suited to a critique-and-negative-results format, the work fits the Findings track, and the failure analysis is the contribution we stand behind. The paper is a critique of verification tooling, not a pipeline-shift detector, and a case-report framing would suit its Type 2 to 4 material.
