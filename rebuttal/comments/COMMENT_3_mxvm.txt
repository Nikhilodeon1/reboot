Reply to Reviewer mxvm.

Your first weakness is the strongest, and we agree: the precision and recall figures describe an internal suite of a few curated, partly correlated cases, not performance on a population. In the revised version we will remove them from the abstract and report per-detector counts with exact intervals in the main table. The pooled row will be marked reference only: precision 6/6 [0.541, 1.000], recall 6/7 [0.421, 0.996], false-positive rate 0/12 [0.000, 0.265]. We will also add a validation-depth table that shows, per detector, how many positives and controls are internal and external, how many controls can fail by a margin rule fixed in advance, and what baseline exists. For detector 1 at full scale we regenerated the labels and archived the observed values. One of six controls is discriminating (the MIMIC-IV control, 0.06 standard deviations from the threshold) and five are ceiling controls (the eICU-CRD control is at 5.39). Detector 4 has no external positive. We will say these limits in the paper.

Your second weakness names three detectors. We added data to each, and we treat them as findings of the audit, not defects we repaired.

Detector 5 fails its positive case. The flagship composition ratio straddles its gate across seeds: it flagged 2/5 at n = 1200, 0/5 at n = 4000, and 3/5 at n = 900 and at n = 1500. A normal approximation with the seed-to-seed spread gives flag probabilities of about 0.42 and 0.15 at the first two sizes, consistent with those counts (five seeds per cell, and the same seeds supply estimate and count, so this is exploratory). Restricted to two components, it flags 5 of 5 but is unstable on its control. On external data with controlled ablation, five seeds, precision 9/9, false-positive rate 0/10.

Detector 3 has no coverage. It recognised none of 727 third-party files, and we reviewed 53 sampled sites by hand: 0 contaminated, 1 disclosed oracle, 1 ambiguous, 21 sound and 30 not selections. Sensitivity is undefined, and the detector returned indeterminate on 53 of 53 files.

Detector 2 cannot separate leakage from missingness. We ran the pre-registered arms that separate them. On the demonstration pair the missingness share was 0.02 and -0.11 at 5% and 20%, so the effect is values-dominated by the rule, with the caveat that our mask arm is under-filled. At full scale the share is undetermined for lack of raw series. We also found a different problem: a corpus-size control recovers 0.55 and 0.69 of the effect at full scale, above our pre-registered line of 0.5, so the effect is confounded with corpus size. Its 0% control compared an arm with itself, so we ran a 20-replicate null: 1 of 20 flagged (exact 95% interval 0.001 to 0.249).

On your request for stronger validation, preferably on external data: we met it only in part. The Type 1 read now covers 25 repositories with exact intervals (4/48 (exact 95% interval 0.023 to 0.200) on one reading and 16/48 (0.204 to 0.484) on the other). We also reran the three standard baselines on the same trained models at demonstration and full scale. We found no external instance of Types 2 to 4, and we will say so in an evidence-strength table. We did not run the missingness arms at full scale, and we ran no GPU work.
