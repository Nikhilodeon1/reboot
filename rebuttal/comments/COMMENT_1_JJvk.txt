Reply to Reviewer JJvk.

We take your main concern first, because it is right: the four failure modes are not supported equally well, and the evidence does not support calling them a general or complete taxonomy. In answer to your first question, we see them as an initial catalogue based on the cases we met in this project. In the revised version we will use that wording ("initial catalogue of failure modes, organized by the stage of a diagnostic at which they arise"), state that completeness is claimed only relative to a stage model, list the classes the catalogue does not cover, and add a table of evidence per type. As submitted, Types 1 to 3 have measured instances in our own detectors, Type 1 also has an external structural instance, and Type 4 is supported only by a counterfactual. We will say exactly that, and we will drop "each type has a measured instance here".

On circularity and detector 3, your second question asked for a manual review of a subset of the repositories. We did it. We enumerated candidate model-selection sites in the 18 repositories with patterns frozen before the scan, sampled 53, and labelled each against a rubric committed before any site was read and before the detector was run on it. The outcome was 0 contaminated, 1 disclosed oracle, 1 ambiguous, 21 sound and 30 not selections. No site was contaminated, so detector 3's sensitivity is undefined, not low: undisclosed contamination was absent in 0 of 23 (exact 95% interval 0.000 to 0.148) real selection sites (the 23 sites lie in 15 files, so they are not independent). The detector returned indeterminate on the files of all of them, so it also makes no clean verdict. We also ran the static leakage tool of Yang et al. on both of detector 3's fixtures. It reports nothing on either and recognises no train-validation-test pairing in them. The fixtures are not in the format that tool targets, so we read that as "does not apply to our idiom", not as a failure. We will describe detector 3 as a guard for one's own sweeps, not a retrospective auditor. An author who had not seen our labels also labelled 12 sampled sites, and then used a language model as a second check on those labels: 8 of 12 labels agreed (exact 95% interval 0.349 to 0.901), and neither labeller found a contaminated site (the global comment gives the disagreements). It is an agreement figure, not a blind relabel, and the limitations will say that the same group built and audited everything.

On detector 2 (your third question), we pre-stated that we expected a mixed result with the missingness share between 0.25 and 0.6. We ran the experiment. On the demonstration pair the share was 0.02 and -0.11 at 5% and 20% leakage, which the pre-registered rule reads as values-dominated, so our prediction was wrong. The mask-transplant arm is under-filled (0.215 of cells observed against 0.301 in the target), so a missingness contribution is not excluded. At full scale the share is undetermined, because the caches we had lack the raw series. A bigger result came from a different control: at full scale a corpus-size control recovers 0.55 and 0.69 of the effect (global comment, item 1). For detector 5, the external component set is analogous, not identical. Restricted to the two components present in every database, it flags the PhysioNet flagship in 5 of 5 seeds, but its ratio on the same-site control is unstable (mean 2.905 at n = 900), so we will report that the composition gate is not reliable there.

On the weight placed on internal precision and recall, and your suggestion about the abstract: we agree. In the revised version the abstract will lead with the failure analysis, drop the pooled precision, false-positive and recall figures, and say in one clause that this is a critique, with negative results, of verification tooling. The main table will give per-detector counts with exact intervals. The pooled row will be marked reference only (6/6 [0.541, 1.000] precision, 6/7 [0.421, 0.996] recall, 0/12 [0.000, 0.265] false-positive rate).
