Global comment, part 1 of 2: Corrections.

Running the experiments the reviews asked for showed five errors in our own claims. We state them here, before anything else, and we will correct each in the revised version.

1. Detector 2's effect is confounded with corpus size at full scale, by our own pre-registered rule. We ran a control that adds extra source-site stays in place of target-site stays. On full-scale MIMIC-IV to eICU-CRD it recovers {{N:r2r1_full5}} of the shipped detector's effect at 5% leakage and {{N:r2r1_full20}} at 20%. At demonstration scale the ratios were {{N:r2r1_demo5}} and {{N:r2r1_demo20}}. Our pre-registered line was 0.5, and both full-scale values are above it. Target exposure still adds beyond volume (paired t of {{N:t_a1a2_full}} for the shipped arm against the control). We left the detector unchanged, so this is a limitation on what its verdict establishes. The detector's 0% control holds corpus size fixed, so it could not have shown this.

2. We retract the statement that detector 1's near-miss was "worse than at 1/640th the data, not better". It compared two different quantities: a cohort-bootstrap flag probability at demonstration scale ({{N:d1_cohort_demo}}) with an audit-draw flag probability at full scale ({{N:d1_audit_full}}). Like for like, the cohort-bootstrap value is {{N:d1_cohort_demo}} at demonstration scale and {{N:d1_cohort_full}} at full scale. What full scale shows is that the population kappa ({{N:d1_pop}}) sits on the 0.60 threshold, so a 500-patient audit is close to a coin flip. Related: by the margin rule we fixed in advance (a control is discriminating if its statistic is within 3 standard deviations of the threshold), only one detector 1 control is discriminating, the MIMIC-IV window-versus-single-point control at {{N:d1_m_mimic}} standard deviations. The eICU-CRD control, which the paper presents as the counterexample, is a ceiling control at {{N:d1_m_eicu}}.

3. Detector 2's 0% control compared an arm with itself, so its true negative held by construction. We then ran an exploratory null: 20 independent paired five-seed comparisons of two no-leak arms, each with a fresh data draw and model seed. {{N:null_flags}} flagged (exact 95% interval {{N:null_ci}}). That is consistent with the nominal 5%, and the upper bound is 25%. The null uses independent model seeds, which is harder than the detector's own shared-seed comparison.

4. We will remove the claim that leakage raises cross-site AUROC, and the signal-to-nuisance ratio built on it. On the full held-out target site of the demonstration pair, the change in AUROC is {{N:a5p_d5}}, so only 100% leakage is distinguishable from zero, by a small margin. At full scale no level is distinguishable from zero: {{N:fs_auroc}} at 5%, 20% and 100% leakage, with {{N:fs_pos}} positives per seed. The exposure detector 2 measures does not produce a measurable downstream change in our setting.

5. We will remove the claim that the falsy-as-clean pattern needs a component that produces a verdict. We read {{N:t4_repos}} repositories and found the pattern reachable and unguarded in {{N:t4_narrow}} of applicable sampled sites, or {{N:t4_broad}} if NaNs that arise only from diverged training count. We did not fix that convention in advance, and we report both readings without choosing between them. Splitting the sites into verdict or selection code and other code gives {{N:t4_split_narrow}} on the first reading and {{N:t4_split_broad}} on the second. The direction reverses with the convention, so we found no evidence that the pattern concentrates in verdict code.

What was not done. We did not run the missingness arms at full scale, because the caches we had lack the raw series they need, so that question is open at full scale. We ran no GPU work, because the credentialed data stayed on one author's machine. The scans of third-party code cover Python files only ({{N:t3_gap}} outside the scan), and third-party tools were built and run only inside Docker.
