Reply to Reviewer hDMD.

Your strongest point is that the failure modes lack formal definitions and an argument for completeness, and that three external repositories do not support generalisation claims. Both are right. On definitions, in the revised version we will add a stage model: a diagnostic has a statistic, a decision rule, controls that validate the pair, and a design process. Each type will get a predicate tied to one stage. Type 1a: the verdict function is total on a domain where the statistic is partial. Type 1b: a control whose margin to the threshold exceeds 3 standard deviations of the statistic, so it cannot fail. Type 2: for two implementations that a domain convention treats as interchangeable, the population value of the statistic lies within one audit standard deviation of the threshold. Type 3: the statistic's aggregation level or pairing differs from the structure of the confound. Type 4: a decision rule changed after observing its failure. We will claim completeness only relative to that stage model, and we will list what the catalogue does not cover (an invalid reference distribution, non-independent audit draws, plain implementation bugs, a threshold miscalibrated for another reason). The definitions are ours and have not been reviewed by outside experts. We will say that, and we welcome specific suggestions on who should review them.

On generalisation, we extended the external read beyond three repositories. It covered {{N:t4_repos}} repositories: the 18 from the detector 3 scan, AIF360 and six new ones chosen by a star-ranked rule fixed before any query. Site patterns were frozen first, and the sampled sites were labelled by a rubric committed before reading ({{N:t4_sites}}). The pattern was reachable and unguarded in {{N:t4_narrow}} of applicable sites, or {{N:t4_broad}} if NaNs that arise only from diverged training count. We did not fix that convention in advance. The four positives are in three files and we will list each with file, line and commit. This external evidence supports Type 1 only, and the revised version will say so. We found nothing external for Types 2 to 4.

On intervals and heterogeneous pooling: we will report per-detector counts with exact (Clopper-Pearson) intervals in the main table, and mark the pooled row reference only, because the cases are heterogeneous and partly correlated: precision {{N:pool_prec}}, recall {{N:pool_rec}}, false-positive rate {{N:pool_fpr}}. The abstract will not use it.

On separating the internal audit from the taxonomy: we will reorder the paper to detectors and protocol, internal validation, the catalogue, evidence per type, then external generality.

On consequences outside our repository: we ran the verbatim DomainBed selection and guard lines on synthetic inputs. A NaN score listed first wins the selection, one listed later is ignored, and both a NaN value and an empty list pass the guard. This shows the semantics of the pattern, not a bug in shipped DomainBed, which cannot reach these inputs with its shipped datasets. We will keep the wording "structurally present, not currently exploitable". We also computed the split between verdict or selection code and other code: {{N:t4_split_narrow}} on the first reading and {{N:t4_split_broad}} on the second. It reverses with the convention, so we will remove the argument that the pattern concentrates in verdict code.

You counted the baseline-shift insight among the strengths. We keep the qualitative point, with a correction: the 0% source-to-target gap is {{N:gap_full}} at full scale and {{N:gap_a5p}} on the demonstration pair, and the leakage-induced change is small, but we will remove the ratio comparison and report the intervals ({{N:a5p_d5}} on the large held-out target).

On the case-report suggestion: we are receptive. Types 2 to 4 are narrower evidence than a general catalogue needs.
