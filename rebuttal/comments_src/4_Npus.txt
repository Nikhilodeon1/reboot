Reply to Reviewer Npus.

Your strongest point is that the baseline comparison was not head to head, and that uneven validation depth may explain the uncomfortable findings for detectors 3 and 5 better than detector-specific failure does. Both are right.

On the scale mismatch: the submission compared detector 2 with the three standard checks on a demonstration cohort, while its main analysis was at full scale. We reran the comparison with detector 2 and the three checks read off the same trained models, stays and seeds, at both scales. At demonstration scale the checks flag at 0% leakage ({{N:base_demo}}). At full scale (flags out of 5 seeds at 0, 5, 20 and 100% leakage), {{N:base_full}}. None of the three carries information about this scenario, in different ways at the two scales. We will not claim that detector 2 beats them: a corpus-size control recovers {{N:r2r1_full5}} and {{N:r2r1_full20}} of detector 2's full-scale effect (global comment, item 1), so a large part of what it detects is data volume. Downstream AUROC does not move at full scale ({{N:fs_auroc}} at 5%, 20% and 100% leakage, with a 0% source-to-target gap of {{N:gap_full}}). We will delete the sentences that said leakage raises cross-site AUROC and shrinks the gap between in-domain and cross-site performance and that the signal-to-nuisance ratio favoured purpose-built detectors.

On uneven validation depth: we will add a table that gives, for each detector, positives and controls internal and external, the number of controls that can fail by a margin rule fixed in advance, the external ground truth, the uncertainty measure, and the baseline and manual-label coverage. For the baselines we have the following. Detector 1: a cross-site prevalence-ratio check, which is inverted, because it ranks legitimate cross-site comparisons above real label mismatches (AUROC for picking out mismatches {{N:prev_auroc_demo}} at demonstration scale and {{N:prev_auroc_full}} at full scale). Detector 3: the static tool of Yang et al., built in Docker after three build changes, which reports nothing on both of our fixtures and recognises no train-validation-test pairing in either, so it does not apply to our idiom. Detector 4: two literature searches found no baseline, and we did not invent one. Detector 5: we will state in the paper that no baseline was run, because the naive cross-site gap is the quantity the detector decomposes, so it is an input and not an independent check. We also added depth to detector 3 (a hand review of {{N:t3_sampled}} sampled sites) and to detector 5 (per-seed archives and a five-seed external run), so the uncomfortable findings now rest on more than a single comparison.

On presentation: we agree that the ordering of results does not follow the ordering of methods. In the revised version the order will be detectors and protocol, internal validation, the catalogue, evidence per type, external generality, then the comparison with standard practice. The internal audit and the definition of the catalogue will be separate sections.

On the summary: you describe the paper as proposing an audit framework to detect silent pipeline shifts. We will not argue with the summary. We will state the contribution in the introduction as a critique, with negative results, of verification tooling: we built five detectors, and the audit of those detectors is the contribution. It is not a method for detecting dataset shift.
