Points in the order of the review.

**Headline rests on 6 true positives and 12 true negatives (Y1).** Conceded. The headline numbers are removed from the abstract. Table 1 reports per-detector counts with exact intervals, and the pooled row is labelled reference only because the cases are heterogeneous and partly correlated: precision 6/6 [0.541, 1.000], recall 6/7 [0.421, 0.996], false-positive rate 0/12 [0.000, 0.265]. We do not read them as performance. The abstract leads with the failure analysis.

**"Every type has a measured failure" is too strong (Y2).** Conceded. The sentence is gone. Table 3 states, per type, the internal instances, the external instances and how each is quantified. Types 1 to 3 have measured instances in our detectors. Type 1 also has an external structural instance: 4/48 (exact 95% interval 0.023 to 0.200) of applicable sites, or 16/48 (0.204 to 0.484) if NaNs that arise only from diverged training count. Type 2 rests on one detector plus an exploratory second. Type 4 is counterfactual. Types 2 to 4 have no external instance.

**Question 1: what shows Type 4 as an observed failure?** Nothing does. Type 4 is a process hazard, and we did not observe it as a failure of a shipped detector. Section 6.4 now says so. The evidence is counterfactual. We pre-registered a rule for choosing between two variants of detector 5. The alternative had higher recall at demonstration scale and was tempting. The pre-registered criterion rejected it. One case with unknown ground truth was excluded from scoring in advance, and it is the only case on which the variants diverge: scored, it alone would have decided the comparison. A separate arm that fitted detector 3's recognition vocabulary to the corpus did not help. We also did not move the detector 1 threshold after seeing the near-miss. This shows what pre-registration prevented, not an error that occurred, and we do not offer it as more than that.

**Question 2: when is a threshold or a control clinically meaningful?** Section 6.2 now answers this. The 0.60 threshold is a conventional agreement benchmark with no clinical derivation. A negative control is informative when it is a pair of operationalizations that a domain expert accepts as interchangeable. A threshold is meaningful only if it sits clear of the legitimate-variation band of such pairs. For detector 1 the same implementation pair gives kappa 0.602 on MIMIC-IV and 0.826 on eICU-CRD, so the band is wide relative to the margin of 0.002 by which the MIMIC control misses the threshold. We state this as a prescription for building new detectors, to be fixed before seeing data. We do not apply it retroactively to detector 1, because moving its threshold now would be the Type 4 error.

**Related changes that bear on your points.** The Type 2 model's flag probability for detector 1 (0.475 against 0.484 observed) is a consistency check, since both come from the same draws. The statement that the near-miss worsened with more data is retracted: the cohort-bootstrap flag probability is 0.216 at demonstration scale and 0.241 at full scale.
